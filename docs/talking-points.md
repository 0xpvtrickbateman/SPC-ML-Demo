# Presenter talking points

Use slides 1–12 for the main narrative. The appendix supports technical questions. All counts and metrics describe the final synthetic run. Use `presentation-runbook.md` for exact notebook actions.

## Slide 1: Daily application volumes SPC and machine learning

Opening: This is a synthetic USCIS-related workload demonstration. Daily receipts and completions represent aggregate operational counts. We look for unusual patterns, route them to an analyst, and separately estimate future volume. No applicant decisions or agency performance claims follow from this demonstration.

## Slide 2: The operational question

Explain the workflow without model terminology first. A workload owner wants to know which recent volume changes deserve attention. A reviewer checks source completeness, calendar effects, workload changes and operational context. The disposition belongs to a human. Future workload planning is a different question.

## Slide 3: Synthetic daily counts

Dataset: attainx_applications_v3_seed42. 1,179,830 synthetic event rows aggregate to 1,260 daily observations across 3 series and 420 business dates. Over a million event rows do not mean over a million independent training examples. The models train on daily-series feature windows. Calendar and generation settings come from the executed notebook. The chart labels Receipts A and Receipts B map to notebook series_id Intake A and Intake B. Completions A retains the same name. There are no applicant records. Do not describe these counts as historical USCIS outcomes.

## Slide 4: Statistical process control (SPC)

SPC compares recent observations with a historical reference. XmR checks large deviations. CUSUM accumulates small departures. EWMA smooths observations to expose shifts. Their OR forms the classifier label. Separate severity zones add point and run rules. Control limits characterize reference variation. They do not establish mission targets or explain causes. Show the notebook rule flags, contributing daily rows and one review episode.

## Slide 5: Each method answers a different question

Keep the distinction explicit. SPC is direct deterministic logic. The classifier learns a proxy label generated by that logic. Isolation Forest learns unusual feature patterns without incident labels. Regression and statistical time-series methods estimate future counts. None determines a root cause or authorizes action.

## Slide 6: Training uses earlier dates

Walk through the measurement features before describing algorithms. During fit, the random forest grows decision trees on sampled training rows and feature subsets. Each tree learns split thresholds that separate the rule labels. Voting across trees gives a class and a vote-based score. The regressor averages tree predictions for counts. A saved model retains those learned splits for inference. Each classification row summarizes a prior window. The label is the OR of explicit rule flags. Chronological splitting prevents later dates from training earlier predictions. A 25-business-day separation prevents overlap of 25-day measurement windows. Earlier 90-day references can overlap. This is not independent incident validation.

## Slide 7: The comparison determines the conclusion

These are executed synthetic-run results. The classification target is the rule label, not a confirmed incident. Report precision and recall with F1, plus the constant baseline. The rolling-fold comparison is a separate experiment from the single holdout. Do not assume that a more complex algorithm wins.

## Slide 8: Forecasting has a separate target

The regressor predicts the next business-day count using history available at forecast time. The five-day comparison evaluates statistical models over multiple origins. Lower mean absolute error is better. Horizons and samples differ, so do not rank one-day and five-day methods against each other. Candidate selection uses earlier origins, with a final origin held aside.

## Slide 9: An analyst owns the disposition

Show one episode and its lineage, source freshness and possible operational events. An automatically proposed hypothesis is a prompt to investigate. The notebook replay contains fictional dispositions and a prepared outbox. It does not send messages or establish real human review. A production reviewer can acknowledge, investigate, request data correction or close with evidence.

## Slide 10: Saved models can score later data

Explain training versus inference. Training learns parameters from the permitted earlier history. A persisted artifact stores the model. Loading it later and calling predict reuses those parameters without fitting again. Demonstrated local persistence and target-platform MLflow logging are separate proof levels.

## Slide 11: Monitoring supports retraining review

Input drift means feature distributions changed. Performance drift means error changes after actual outcomes arrive. Either can motivate investigation. Neither proves the cause. Demonstration thresholds are illustrative. A controlled exercise perturbs outcomes while retaining predictions to show watch and review states. No automatic retraining or deployment occurs.

## Slide 12: What this demonstration establishes

Close by separating runnable demonstration capability from production acceptance. We can demonstrate the pipeline and decision records on synthetic data. A pilot needs approved representative data, a workload owner, outcome definitions and operating thresholds. Run it prospectively in shadow mode before promoting a model. No labor savings, real-world accuracy or historical agency outcomes are established.

## Slide 13: Appendix A   Data and feature contract

Technical reference. The daily table contains aggregate synthetic workload counts. Subgroup rows must reconcile to their parent. A rolling measurement row is not a daily-count row. Preserve time semantics across daily counts, features, episodes and forecasts.

## Slide 14: Appendix B   Model training details

All settings here match the notebook. The holdout classifier and rolling-fold candidates use different settings, so keep their experiments separate. Logistic regression standardizes inputs. Isolation Forest does not train on incident labels and its training-derived contamination heuristic does not reveal a true incident rate.

## Slide 15: Appendix C   Validation and uncertainty

Describe limitations before generalizing results. Synthetic labels and generation patterns are simplified. Chronological tests support ordering but do not establish broad external validity. Class imbalance makes accuracy insufficient. Prediction intervals and calibration require separate assessment where absent. Five-day subgroup forecasts are independent and need not reconcile to the aggregate.

## Slide 16: Appendix D   Deployment and monitoring

This slide describes the intended production lifecycle, not verified live deployment. Daily jobs score new data with a versioned approved model, write appendable predictions, and wait for actual outcomes. Dashboards read persisted tables. An owner reviews alert burden and model performance. Data correction precedes retraining when appropriate.

## Slide 17: Appendix E   Scope and boundaries

Use these answers consistently. This is an aggregate workload demonstration, not case adjudication. It detects statistical patterns without proving operational cause. Synthetic experiments cannot demonstrate agency accuracy or labor savings. Local checks do not prove a scheduled Databricks job, delivered alert, live registry promotion or real operator acceptance.
